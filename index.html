Finding 01Oct – Nov 2026Data quality · Time series · Statistics

US grid forecasts looked like they were getting worse. Three errors in the public data explain it.

Every day, the organizations that run the US power grid forecast tomorrow’s electricity demand, and the federal Energy Information Administration (EIA) publishes those forecasts next to what actually happened. Taken at face value, the national numbers say forecasting is getting worse. I audited ten years of that data and traced the apparent decline to three errors in what was published. Once they’re fixed, the trend is flat.

Under the hood

6.1 million hourly records for 50+ operators from EIA’s hourly grid data (EIA-930), an analysis plan committed to Git before any results, and 138 automated tests.

As published

The national number says forecasts got worse.

Across the 50 operators present every year, national forecast error creeps up by 0.061 percentage points a year (Mann–Kendall p = 0.049), and 2025 is the worst of the ten years.

But the typical operator shows no clear trend, and three of the five robustness checks I committed to in advance don’t confirm the rise. So, as the plan required, I checked the largest operators that lose to the naive forecast against their own published data. In both that could be checked, the published data had errors.

Error 1 and 2 · California ISO

California’s demand ran an hour late.

In EIA’s data, the California ISO’s forecast matches the operator’s own, but its demand runs an hour behind the operator’s records, so the forecast looks worse than it is. That holds on 333 of 334 days checked in 2025, and for about four of the ten years in all.

It also counts electricity used to charge grid batteries, which the forecast leaves out. In 2025 the gap between forecast and demand follows battery charging almost megawatt for megawatt (R² = 0.91).

Error 3 · Southwest Power Pool

Southwest Power Pool’s forecast changed in May 2025.

Before 7 May 2025, the forecast in EIA’s data for Southwest Power Pool matched SPP’s own day-ahead forecast (within 2% on 122 of 125 days). After that, the two were more than 2% apart on 219 of 238 days, while the demand figures still agreed. The jump in error comes from the published forecast, not from SPP’s forecasting. Why it changed isn’t visible in the files.

Corrected

With the three errors fixed, the trend is flat.

National error now moves just 0.009 points a year (p = 0.721, no detectable trend), and 2025 falls from the worst of the ten years to 8th-worst. California’s 2025 error drops from 8.28% to 2.35%.

Limits

Where the audit stops.

The corrections came after the pre-registered results, and only operators that looked bad were checked against their own data, which tilts the corrected trend down. The write-up says both, along with what is still open.

Finding 02Aug 2026Vision-language · Evaluation

Grounding DINO + SAM finds “the dog.” It mostly misses “not.”

Some AI tools can find and outline an object in a photo from a typed request like “the dog,” and they’re used to label data and edit images automatically. I tested whether they understand the small words that change the meaning: “the dog that is not on the sofa,” “the cup left of the plate,” “the red one.” With “not” and “left of” they mostly fail, and I pinned the failures to one part of the system.

Under the hood

I built a benchmark of 914 human-verified test prompts across 608 images from COCO, LVIS and Visual Genome, covering negation, spatial relations, attribute binding and fine-grained categories. Grounding DINO + SAM scored 20.7% on negation, below chance, against 93% on control prompts. On 51 left/right pairs it never got both right: it picks the same object whichever side is asked for. Given the correct boxes, SAM scored 99.6%, so every failure is in reading the text, not in drawing the outline.

Finding 03Feb 2026MLOps · Production ML

Models fail quietly when the data shifts. This pipeline notices, retrains, and checks before it ships.

A model that was accurate at launch can slowly go wrong as the world it sees changes, and nothing crashes to warn you. I built a system around a model that predicts which bank customers will sign up for a term deposit: it watches for that kind of change, retrains the model, and only lets the new model take over if it’s actually better.

Under the hood

An XGBoost model served behind FastAPI, every prediction logged to PostgreSQL, drift checks with Evidently, Prometheus and Grafana, and retraining run by Airflow and tracked in MLflow. A retrained model only ships if it gains at least 0.005 PR-AUC on shifted data without losing more than 0.03 ROC-AUC on historical data. In a simulated recession, retraining lifted PR-AUC on the shifted data from 0.84 to 0.89 and ROC-AUC from 0.54 to 0.65, and the new model passed the historical check (ROC-AUC 0.75).

Finding 04Dec 2025Computer vision · Medical imaging · Team of 2

Spotting lung CT scans that AI has tampered with.

AI can now edit medical scans, adding a lung tumor that isn’t there or erasing one that is, which could change a diagnosis. As a team of two, we trained models to flag the parts of a lung CT scan that have been altered.

Under the hood

PyTorch models trained on a public dataset of 100 lung CT scans with 169 labeled locations, where a GAN had injected or removed tumors. Our 3D-to-2D compressor condenses 16 CT slices into one image for an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18, and it ranked first of five designs (by best and average validation AUC) on 39 patches from scans held out of training. With no separate test set, that shows a learnable signal, not a finished detector, and the repository says so.

In progressLLM evaluation · Information extraction

Can an LLM read an earnings release without getting the numbers wrong?

Every quarter, companies announce their results in press releases, and pulling the key numbers out of them is routine work in finance. I’m testing whether AI models can do it without mistakes, checking every answer automatically against what the company officially files with the SEC.

Under the hood

Models extract revenue, net income and diluted EPS from 60 earnings press releases, and all 293 figures are graded against the company’s XBRL filing. Three API models scored 293 / 293. Open models mostly failed on units: the right figure, off by a power of ten. Asking for each figure as printed plus its unit, and doing the arithmetic in code, cut their errors 65% and unit slips 90%. Now: a LoRA fine-tune of Qwen3-8B, served with vLLM.
